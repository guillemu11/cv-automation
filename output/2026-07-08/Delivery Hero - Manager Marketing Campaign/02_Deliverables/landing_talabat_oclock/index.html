Quick-commerce demand clusters into a handful of predictable peaks — lunch, dinner, late-night. Everyone competes there, so the margin is thin and the message is noise. The real growth for talabat mart sits in the twenty hours between the peaks: the 3pm slump, the pre-match snack run, the 45°C afternoon, the payday haul. Each is a craving with a clock. Talabat O'Clock turns the calendar itself into the media plan.

The idea

talabat mart already lives in the moment — whatever you need, in twenty minutes. So the campaign shouldn't be a burst. It should be a clock.

One always-on framework, endlessly reskinnable, where creative, basket and push notification fire against real-world triggers: the Hijri calendar, the weather API, the football fixture list, the payday window, the time on the phone in your hand.

Same platform, infinite editions. Ramadan, matchday, heatwave, payday, the 3pm karak. Every edition says the same thing in a new outfit: whatever you're craving, it's talabat o'clock.

How it runs — the always-on calendar

The engine

One clock, real-time triggers

A moment-planning layer wires each craving to a signal — time of day, Hijri date, live weather, kick-off, salary window, geo. When a trigger fires, the matching creative + mart basket + push goes live automatically. The campaign never sleeps; it just changes shift.

Events Mode — when the city shows up

The calendar's biggest spikes aren't daily — they're the nights the whole city is out. Global Village, the F1 weekend, a stadium concert, Comic Con, National Day. Talabat O'Clock geofences the venue and the hours around it: pre-event supply runs before you leave home, doorstep restocks for the house party after. One framework, scaled up to the moments people plan their week around — with creator partnerships and event-sponsor tie-ins baked in.
